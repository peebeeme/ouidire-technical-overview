# Analytical method

## Evidence before synthesis

OuiDire's core methodological choice is to keep an inspectable evidence layer between raw documents and higher-level interpretation.

```text
source document
  → reconstructed structure
  → source-linked cards
  → machine suggestions
  → human validation and notes
  → document/corpus synthesis
  → traceable export
```

## Four analytical levels

1. **Card** — the atomic evidence unit.
2. **Document** — recurring mechanisms and local structure.
3. **Corpus** — propagation, contradiction, and patterns across documents.
4. **Thesis** — an explanatory model assembled from reviewable findings.

This hierarchy prevents a corpus-level narrative from becoming detached from the material it purports to explain.

## Complementary roles

| Role | Question | Typical output |
|---|---|---|
| Orientation | Where should the reviewer look first? | provisional thesis, clusters, priorities |
| Structured analysis | Which defined mechanisms may be present? | card-linked suggestions with rationale |
| Human evidence work | What is accepted, rejected, or qualified? | annotations and explicit decisions |
| Focused critical review | What deserves deeper examination? | bounded findings linked to reviewed evidence |
| Synthesis | What pattern emerges across the record? | document/corpus overview and export |

## Public case-model references

Terms such as **OAS** and labels such as **P-1 to P-10** describe document types or exhibit identifiers used in demonstrations and evaluation scenarios; they are not model names. Public examples must be synthetic and must not reproduce real filings or personal records.

## Proprietary layer

OuiDire uses specialized analytical vocabularies and rules. Their existence, purpose, and evaluation can be documented publicly, while their complete definitions, prompts, weights, and implementation remain proprietary. This is deliberate: transparency about system behaviour does not require publishing every production control.

## Responsible interpretation

Outputs are decision support for qualified human review. They may contain errors and should not be treated as diagnoses, legal opinions, or autonomous findings. A conclusion that cannot be traced to source material should be treated as unverified.
